New Trade Compatible Products

#### BA - Bankers Acceptance

- A financing for both Buyers and Sellers pre/post-shipment (benefits both buyer and seller) (Usance LC, D/A Collection).
- In LC, the buyer can only use it if the payment term is Usance LC.
- In Trade Collection, can only be used if the agreed on D/A.

Seller's side

- Seller gets bank guarantee (safer than buyer's promise)
- Use when the seller wants to discount BA for immediate cash (don't wait 90 days) or wants to have a bank guaranteed maturity payment.
- Use when the seller wants stronger payment security than just LC issuing bank
- Use when the seller still considering if they want immediate cash or want to wait for LC maturity
- Use when the seller is concerned about waiting 90 days for payment from the buyer.

Buyer's side

- Buyer reimburses bank at maturity (not immediately)
- Bank provides the acceptance/guarantee to seller

- Note: It's primarily used when sellers want enhanced payment security and flexibility in cash flow timing. The seller typically drives the decision to use BA based on their risk tolerance and liquidity needs.

#### BG - Bank Guarantee

A service/guarantee/risk mitigation tool for both Buyers and Sellers. (Trade LC, Collection, OA/standalone)

- Trade: Supports contracts in LC, Collections, Open Account
- Standalone: Construction, tenders, general business contracts

- Bank promises to pay a third party if the customer fails to perform/pay.
- Bank backs up the buyer’s promises across any trade method or standalone business situation.

#### CBP - Clean Bill Purchased

A financing for Sellers post-shipment timing. (Collection D/A, OA)

- Seller ships goods → Creates payment bill → Bank buys bill for cash, giving the seller instant money → Bank collects from buyer later
- Seller sell their bill (IOU) to the bank for instant money.
- Bank purchases bills of exchange (without documents of title to goods attached). Seller gets immediate funds.

#### DRF - Direct Recourse Financing

A financing for Sellers post-shipment timing. (Collection, OA)

- Seller gets cash, but if the buyer doesn’t pay, the bank advances funds to the seller. If the buyer doesn’t pay, the seller must refund the bank.
- Bank gives the seller money in advance.
- If the buyer doesn’t pay, the seller must refund the bank.
- Get the money now but if the buyer doesn’t pay, the risk returns to the seller.

#### ECR - Export Credit Refinancing

A financing (via central bank to commercial bank) for Sellers Pre-shipment. (LC, standalone)

- A government-subsidized financing for Sellers pre-shipment (LC, standalone)
- Central bank/export agency provides cheap funds to commercial banks
- Commercial banks pass on cheaper rates to exporters

- Central bank (or export agency) provides refinancing to commercial banks that give credit to exporters.
- Central bank refinances local banks.
- Cheaper credit for exporters.
- “Government helps banks so banks help exporters.”

#### FX - Foreign Exchange

A service for Buyers and Sellers Pre/Post-shipment. (financing add-on for LC, Collection, OA)

- Currency conversion for cross-border trade settlements
- Two types: Spot FX (immediate) or Forward FX (future rate lock)
- Use when the seller and the buyer is using different currency
- Currency transactions; used in settlement of trade flows.
- Buying/selling currencies.
- Needed in cross-border trade.
- “Trade in dollars, pay in baht = FX bridge.”

#### OFCL - Onshore Foreign Currency Loan

A financing for Buyers. (financing add-on for LC, Collection, OA)

​​- No FX conversion risk - borrow and repay in same foreign currency

- Natural hedge against currency movements
- Local bank provides loan in foreign currency needed for imports
- Financing in foreign currency provided domestically, often for imports/exports.
- Local loan, but in foreign currency (e.g., USD).
- Used for import/export financing.
- “Borrow foreign money at home to trade abroad.”

#### Back-to-Back LC

- A financing/guarantee for Sellers who act as middlemen who don't manufacture. Pre-shipment timing
- Seller uses the original LC they agreed with the buyer as security to get the bank to issue another LC to the supplier.
- Use when original LC is NOT transferable.
- Use when the Seller needs more control.
- Use when the LC terms need to be different (amounts, dates, documents)

#### Transferable LC = LC Transferring

#### Supplier Financing

A service/financing for Buyers. (OA)

- Buyers arrange the program, suppliers receive the financing.
- Use when buyer wants to extend payment terms but suppliers need cash flow.
- Use when the buyer wants to strengthen supplier relationships. (so the buyer is the one request for financing).
- Use when buyer has strong credit rating that suppliers lack.
- Use when buyer wants to improve working capital management.
- Help suppliers while extending terms.
- "How many suppliers do you work with regularly?"
- "Would your suppliers benefit from early payment?"
- Help upstream partners (suppliers).

#### Distributor Financing

A financing for Sellers/Suppliers Post-shipment timing before the buyer pays. (OA)

- Sellers arrange the program, buyers receive the financing.
- "Do your buyers (who buy from you for resale) need financing?"
- Help downstream partners (buyers/distributors).

#### NRF - Non-Recourse Financing (Trade/Standalone)

A financing for Sellers Post-shipment timing. (If in trade, Collection, most common in OA)

- Bank finances receivables and assumes the risk of buyer default (exporter has no liability).
- Bank buys your future payment (the bank takes buyer risk)
- "We want immediate cash from our receivables but don't want to worry about buyer default"
